# Video Script Template

This is the definitive format reference for {{PROJECT_NAME}} video scripts. Scripts are talking-points outlines — NOT teleprompter scripts, NOT blog posts read aloud.

## What a Talking-Points Script IS

- Structured outline with key phrases, not full sentences
- Bullet points, transition cues, and ad-lib markers
- A map that guides delivery, not a script that dictates it
- Flexible enough for natural conversation, structured enough for pacing

## What a Talking-Points Script IS NOT

- A word-for-word teleprompter script (never write full paragraphs for reading)
- A blog post reformatted with timestamps
- A transcript of what you'll say — it's a guide for what you'll cover

## Format Example

```markdown
## [SECTION TITLE] (~duration)

[ENGAGEMENT MARKER if applicable]

- Key point or phrase to hit
- "Exact quote or phrase worth saying verbatim" ← [KEY PHRASE]
- Concept to explain in own words
  - Supporting detail
  - Example: [specific tool/number/scenario]

[AD-LIB: personal story about failing at this]

→ Transition: [how to bridge to next section]
```

## Required Script Sections

### 1. COLD OPEN / HOOK (~0:00–0:30)

The first 8 seconds determine if someone stays. This is NOT an intro — it's a reason to watch.

**Must include:**
- Curiosity gap — pose a question or contradiction
- Promise — what they'll know/be able to do by the end
- Proof — why you're credible on this (specific result, number, experience)

**Format:**
```markdown
## COLD OPEN (~0:00–0:30)

[HOOK]

- Curiosity gap: [question or contradiction]
- Promise: [what viewer gets]
- Proof: [specific result/number]

→ Transition: quick context setup
```

### 2. INTRO / CONTEXT (~0:30–1:30)

Orient the viewer. Who is this for, why now, what to expect.

**Must include:**
- Who it's for (and who it's NOT for)
- Personal connection — why you made this video
- Set expectations — what format, how long, what they'll walk away with
- Open loop — tease something coming later

**Format:**
```markdown
## INTRO (~0:30–1:30)

- Who this is for: [audience]
- Why I'm making this: [personal connection]
- What to expect: [format/structure preview]

[OPEN LOOP: tease payoff coming in section X]

→ Transition: let's set the foundation
```

### 3. SETUP / FOUNDATION (~1:30–variable)

Prerequisites and context. Keep this SHORT — viewers came for the core content, not the preamble.

**Rules:**
- Maximum 2 minutes for setup
- Only include what's absolutely necessary to follow the core content
- Link to prerequisite videos rather than re-explaining
- If setup exceeds 2 minutes, you're front-loading too much

### 4. CORE CONTENT (variable — bulk of video)

The main value. Break into sub-sections, each with its own duration target.

**Per sub-section:**
- Concept → Demo/Example → Result
- Include at least one failure or wrong turn
- Specific numbers, tool names, real scenarios

**Format:**
```markdown
## [SUB-SECTION TITLE] (~duration)

- Concept: [what and why]
- Demo: [what to show on screen]
  - Step 1: [specific action]
  - Step 2: [specific action]
- Result: [what happened, specific numbers]

[AD-LIB: where I got stuck doing this]

[PATTERN INTERRUPT: change pace/energy here]

→ Transition: [bridge to next sub-section]
```

### 5. THE PAYOFF / RESULT (~1:00–2:00)

Show the finished result. This is the promise delivered.

**Must include:**
- The actual result (show it, don't just describe it)
- Specific numbers (time saved, cost reduced, output quality)
- Honest assessment — was it worth it? What would you do differently?

### 6. RECAP + CTA (~0:30–1:00)

**Must include:**
- 3-bullet recap of key takeaways (no more)
- Single CTA — one action, not three
- Tease next video if part of a series

**Format:**
```markdown
## RECAP + CTA (~0:30–1:00)

- Recap bullet 1
- Recap bullet 2
- Recap bullet 3

[CTA: single action — subscribe / try it / comment]

- Tease: [next video topic if applicable]
```

## Engagement Markers

Use these inline markers throughout the script. The Script Editor adds and refines them.

| Marker | Purpose | Frequency |
|--------|---------|-----------|
| `[HOOK]` | Reason to keep watching — curiosity, promise, proof | Opening only |
| `[OPEN LOOP: description]` | Tease something coming later to maintain curiosity | Every 3–5 min |
| `[PATTERN INTERRUPT: type]` | Change pace, energy, or format to reset attention | Every 3–4 min |
| `[RETENTION RISK]` | Flag likely viewer drop-off point — needs energy boost | As needed |
| `[CTA: action]` | Call to action — only at high-engagement moments | 1–2 per video |
| `[AD-LIB: topic]` | Zone for personal story, failure, improvisation | 2–4 per video |
| `[KEY PHRASE: "exact words"]` | Specific phrase worth saying verbatim | As needed |
| `[B-ROLL CUE: description]` | Visual that should appear during this section | As needed |

### Pattern Interrupt Types

- **energy shift** — change speaking pace or volume
- **question** — ask viewer a direct question
- **analogy** — unexpected comparison
- **demo switch** — cut to screen, tool, or visual
- **story** — personal anecdote
- **counter-intuitive** — challenge an assumption

## Timing Guidelines

### Short Video (5–8 min)

- 3–4 core sections
- Tight focus — one concept, one demo, one result
- Setup under 1 minute
- Best for: tool tips, quick wins, single technique

### Standard Video (10–15 min)

- 5–7 core sections
- Room for context + demo + result
- Setup up to 1:30
- Best for: tutorials, process walkthroughs, comparisons

### Deep Dive (20–30 min)

- 8–12 core sections
- Full exploration with multiple demos
- Setup up to 2:00
- Best for: comprehensive guides, workshop-style, multi-tool workflows

### Duration Math

Every section MUST have a target duration. Sum of all section durations should land within the target range:
- Short: 5–8 min total
- Standard: 10–15 min total
- Deep dive: 20–30 min total

## YouTube Retention Patterns

### Front-Load Value

Deliver the first piece of real value within 90 seconds. Viewers decide to stay or leave early — give them a reason to stay before you've "set the table."

### The 30% Rule

By the 30% mark of your video, the viewer should have received at least one complete, usable takeaway. If your video is 15 minutes, something actionable by minute 4:30.

### Curiosity Stacking

Don't resolve all open loops at once. As you close one, open another. The viewer should always have a reason to keep watching beyond the current section.

### Sag Point Mitigation

Every video has a natural sag point around 40–60% through. Plan your most engaging content (best demo, biggest reveal, strongest story) for this zone.

### End Screen Window

YouTube's end screen overlay appears in the last 20 seconds. Your CTA and tease should complete before that window starts. Structure accordingly:
- Final words at ~0:30 before end
- Last 20 seconds: natural pause or recap visual for end screen overlay

## Voice Adaptation for Video

the creator'''s written patterns translate to spoken delivery differently:

| Written Pattern | Video Adaptation |
|----------------|-----------------|
| Parenthetical asides `(Yeah...)` | Verbal aside — lower volume, slight pause before/after |
| Ellipses `...` | Actual pause — 1-2 seconds of silence |
| Single-line paragraphs | Natural breath breaks between thoughts |
| Sentence fragments | Quick, punchy delivery — don't pad them |
| Specific numbers | Slow down slightly when saying numbers — let them land |
| "Builders" | Say it naturally — don't over-emphasize |
| Real failures | Lean into the emotion — don't rush past the embarrassment |
| Rhetorical questions | Pause AFTER the question — give viewer time to think |

### Spoken-Only Patterns (not in written content)

- **Direct address:** "Pause the video and try this" / "Let me know in the comments"
- **Screen narration:** "Watch what happens when I..." / "See that error? That's..."
- **Energy modulation:** Sections should alternate between high-energy and conversational
- **Callback:** Reference something from earlier — "Remember when I said...?"
